This literature review synthesizes 285 peer-reviewed publications (2014–2026) on edge-assisted, trust-adaptive misbehavior detection and trusted event dissemination in Vehicular Ad Hoc Networks (VANETs). The review examines trust management models, machine learning-based misbehavior
detection, edge/RSU architectures, SDN integration, sensor-based hazard detection, and simulation methodologies. Key findings reveal that hybrid trust models combining direct observation and indirect reputation achieve 86–95% detection accuracy, while federated learning and blockchain
approaches address privacy concerns. However, significant gaps persist in real-world validation, lightweight edge-deployable ML models, adaptive trust mechanisms for dynamic attack scenarios, and integrated frameworks combining trust management with secure event dissemination. This review
identifies critical research opportunities in RSU misbehavior detection, SDN-based resilient control planes, and sensor-fusion approaches for pothole detection, providing a roadmap for developing comprehensive VANET security solutions.

Vehicular Ad Hoc Networks (VANETs) form the communication backbone of Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), enabling vehicles to exchange safety-critical information about accidents, congestion, road hazards, and emergency events. However, the open and dynamic nature of VANETs
exposes them to diverse security threats, including false data injection, Sybil attacks, message replay, selfish forwarding, and compromised infrastructure nodes. These attacks can have catastrophic consequences, from traffic disruptions to life-threatening accidents.
Trust management and misbehavior detection have emerged as essential security mechanisms to ensure the reliability of disseminated information. Traditional centralized approaches face scalability challenges in highly mobile networks with thousands of vehicles. Recent advances in edge
computing, where Roadside Units (RSUs) provide computational resources near the network edge, offer promising solutions for real-time, low-latency misbehavior detection. Additionally, Software-Defined Networking (SDN) architectures enable flexible, programmable control planes that can adapt
to evolving attack patterns.
This comprehensive literature review examines the state-of-the-art in edge-assisted, trust-adaptive misbehavior detection and trusted event dissemination in VANETs. We analyze 285 publications spanning trust models, machine learning techniques, edge architectures, SDN integration, sensor-
based hazard detection, and simulation methodologies. Our goal is to identify effective approaches, highlight critical gaps, and provide a concrete roadmap for developing integrated VANET security solutions that combine trust management, misbehavior detection, and secure event dissemination.

This paper proposes the D-CASBR (Decentralized Consortium Authentication and Security-Based Reputation) framework for V2X networks, integrating hybrid machine learning (ElasticNet and Gradient Boosting) for real-time anomaly detection, consortium blockchain for secure information
exchange, and a fog-enabled reputation system for trust management. The framework achieves 95% anomaly detection accuracy with minimal false positives, addressing latency issues and eliminating single points of failure. This work is highly relevant as it combines multiple security mechanisms
(ML, blockchain, reputation) in a decentralized edge architecture, directly aligning with our project's goals of edge-assisted, trust-adaptive misbehavior detection.
Relevance to Project: Provides a comprehensive framework integrating ML-based detection, blockchain-based trust, and fog/edge computing—all core components of our proposed system.
Zheng et al. address the dual challenges of rapid anomaly detection and privacy-preserving notification in VANETs through their two-layer privacy-aware trust evaluation CIDS framework (2PT-CIDS). The system employs game-theoretic information incentive mechanisms (Stackelberg and
noncooperative games) and reinforcement learning (reward-shaping double duelling deep Q network) to prevent privacy leakage while enhancing detection. The integration of game theory with deep reinforcement learning represents a novel approach to balancing security and privacy in
collaborative intrusion detection systems.
Relevance to Project: Demonstrates advanced privacy-preserving mechanisms for collaborative detection, addressing a critical concern in trust-based systems where vehicles must share information without compromising privacy.
This comprehensive survey classifies misbehavior detection schemes for VANETs based on architecture (centralized, distributed, hybrid), approach (cryptographic, trust-based, ML-based), and data characteristics. It highlights that machine learning techniques, particularly using the VeReMi
dataset, achieve high accuracy (97–99% for various classifiers), but most techniques lack real-time implementation and suffer from communication overhead due to digital signatures and certificates. The survey identifies the need for compact certificates, data aggregation, and practical real-time
deployment.
Relevance to Project: Provides a comprehensive taxonomy of existing approaches, identifies critical gaps (real-time implementation, overhead reduction), and validates the importance of ML-based detection methods.
The AIT (AI-enabled Trust Management) system combines deep learning for vehicle trust management with blockchain technology for validating reports and enabling RSU-based revocation of untrustworthy vehicles. The system addresses security challenges from erroneous or tampered traffic
messages caused by malfunctioning sensors or malicious vehicles. Experimental results show AIT outperforms existing approaches in accuracy and efficiency, though specific quantitative metrics are not detailed in available metadata.
Relevance to Project: Demonstrates the integration of AI/deep learning with blockchain for trust management and RSU-based enforcement, providing a model for combining detection with revocation mechanisms.
Ahmed et al. propose a blockchain-based authentication scheme to protect vehicle identity privacy and a trust management model that evaluates both vehicle and data trustworthiness to detect false information. The dual evaluation approach (vehicle trust and data trust) enables RSUs to
broadcast only legitimate events. Simulation validation shows better performance than state-of-the-art models in identifying malicious vehicles.
Relevance to Project: Addresses both authentication and trust management with blockchain, providing insights into privacy-preserving identity management and dual-layer trust evaluation for secure event dissemination.
This work proposes a lightweight, attack-resistant trust model using a pseudonym scheme for privacy preservation. The Trust Estimation Model evaluates five parameters: Location Closeness, Data Integrity, Authentication, Time Stamp Verification, and Peer Alert Message. RSUs serve as trusted
sources, and a Decision Model processes or discards messages based on a threshold. Evaluated using SUMO, OMNeT++, and VEINS with OpenStreetMap data, the model achieves a 95% F-score with 40% MiTM attackers, outperforming the MARINE system (90% F-score).
Relevance to Project: Demonstrates a practical, lightweight trust model with specific trust parameters and RSU-based trusted infrastructure, validated with realistic simulation tools (SUMO + OMNeT++ + VEINS).
Abhishek et al. address the critical problem of detecting compromised RSUs in vehicular edge computing environments. Their trust-based detection system collects feedback from vehicles about RSU behavior (packet corruption, dropping) and aggregates trust values at a central trusted server for
threshold-based classification. The system also includes a mechanism to detect malicious vehicles reporting false feedback, addressing the challenge of adversarial feedback in trust systems.
Relevance to Project: Directly addresses RSU misbehavior detection, a critical gap in most VANET security research that focuses primarily on vehicle misbehavior. Provides insights into feedback aggregation and adversarial feedback detection.
The Hybrid Reputation System with Dynamic Update Interval (HDRS) allows separate evaluations by vehicles and RSUs, incorporates a reliability evaluation module to filter unreliable results, and uses a dynamic adjustment mechanism for reputation update intervals based on Analytic Hierarchy
Process (AHP) and reliability. HDRS increases detection rates for collusion attacks by 30% and intelligent attacks by 16% compared to existing schemes, demonstrating effectiveness against sophisticated attack strategies.
Relevance to Project: Introduces dynamic adaptation mechanisms for reputation updates, addressing the challenge of adapting to evolving attack patterns—a key requirement for trust-adaptive systems.
The Multidimensional Trust Evidence Fusion and Path-Backtracking Mechanism (MEFPB) uses Dempster-Shafer theory to fuse direct trust, indirect trust, and message transmission path as trust indicators. The novel inclusion of transmission path as a trust indicator enables more comprehensive
trust assessment. A path-backtracking mechanism identifies and traces malicious behaviors, achieving a high malicious behavior detection rate.
Relevance to Project: Introduces a novel trust indicator (transmission path) and provides a mechanism for tracing attack sources, which is valuable for forensic analysis and adaptive response.
This work proposes a trust-based scheme for detecting malicious RSUs in edge-enabled VANETs using stable and frequent RSU-to-RSU (R2R) interaction to analyze RSU behavior. It includes a mechanism to detect alteration of sensor-detected data in beacon content. Evaluated using SUMO,
NetEdit, and NS-3 with 25 RSUs and 500 vehicles in a 14×14 km area, the system achieves 92% recall and 86% accuracy, though with a notable False Positive Rate up to 30% due to malicious vehicles and RSUs.
Relevance to Project: Provides detailed simulation setup and quantitative results for RSU misbehavior detection, including realistic scenarios with varying percentages of malicious RSUs (20–60%) and vehicles (5–20%).
Zhang et al. investigate trust-based multiservice delivery in fog-enabled VANETs with integrated misbehavior detection and tolerance. The system uses fog computing to analyze and store data for evaluating dynamic trust weights based on vehicle attributes. A trust-aware heterogeneous traffic
allocation algorithm distributes traffic over multiple routing paths with differential resource allocation for misbehavior tolerance. The system achieves 12–40% higher successful service delivery and 10–20% higher effective network utility.

Relevance to Project: Introduces digital twin concepts for vehicular networks and provides time/space-aware anomaly detection mechanisms, offering a novel architectural approach for edge-assisted detection.

Relevance to Project: Demonstrates the application of transfer learning and reinforcement learning (Q-learning) for adaptive trust estimation in crowdsensing scenarios, providing insights into ML-based adaptive trust mechanisms.

Relevance to Project: Demonstrates fuzzy logic for trust evaluation and RSU-based cluster management, providing an alternative to probabilistic trust models.

Trust management forms the foundation of secure VANET communication, enabling vehicles and infrastructure to assess the reliability of received information and the trustworthiness of message sources. The literature reveals several distinct trust modeling approaches:
Direct Trust Models: These models compute trust based on direct observations of node behavior. Junejo et al. [6] propose a five-parameter trust estimation model evaluating Location Closeness, Data Integrity, Authentication, Time Stamp Verification, and Peer Alert Messages. Direct trust models
offer simplicity and low computational overhead but are limited by sparse interactions in highly mobile VANETs.
Indirect Trust Models (Reputation-Based): Reputation systems aggregate trust information from multiple sources. The HDRS system [8] implements a hybrid approach allowing separate evaluations by vehicles and RSUs, with a reliability evaluation module to filter unreliable results. The system
dynamically adjusts reputation update intervals based on Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and reliability metrics, achieving 30% higher detection rates for collusion attacks and 16% higher for intelligent attacks compared to existing schemes.
Multidimensional Trust Fusion: Advanced trust models fuse multiple trust dimensions. Lam et al. [9] propose the MEFPB scheme using Dempster-Shafer theory to fuse direct trust, indirect trust, and message transmission path as indicators. The inclusion of transmission path as a trust indicator
represents a novel contribution, enabling more comprehensive trust assessment and path-backtracking for malicious behavior tracing.
Context-Aware Trust: The CARES system [20] introduces context-aware trust estimation for real-time crowdsensing services, using transfer learning to quickly obtain accurate trust values without prior vehicle knowledge. Q-learning-based dynamic adjustment enables autonomous detection of
malicious vehicles and filtering of untrustworthy input, demonstrating the effectiveness of reinforcement learning for adaptive trust management.
Blockchain-Based Trust: Several works integrate blockchain technology for tamper-proof trust management. Zhang et al. [4] combine deep learning for vehicle trust management with blockchain for validating reports and enabling RSU-based revocation. Ahmed et al. [5] propose blockchain-based
authentication to protect vehicle identity privacy alongside a trust model evaluating both vehicle and data trustworthiness. While blockchain provides strong security guarantees, concerns about scalability and latency remain.
Fuzzy Logic Trust: Fuzzy logic models handle uncertainty in trust assessment. Alkhalidy et al. [22] employ a fuzzy logic model with multiple factors for trust evaluation in RSU-managed clusters. Shahariar et al. [14] propose a fuzzy RSU controller determining rewards or punishments based on
incident severity, driver past behavior, and RSU confidence, demonstrating the effectiveness of adaptive incentive mechanisms.
Key Findings: Hybrid trust models combining direct observation with indirect reputation achieve the highest detection rates (86–95% accuracy). Dynamic adaptation mechanisms (HDRS [8], CARES [20]) significantly improve resilience against intelligent and collusion attacks. However, most trust
models lack real-world validation and do not adequately address the computational overhead of trust computation in resource-constrained vehicular environments.
Misbehavior detection mechanisms identify and mitigate malicious behavior in VANETs, ranging from false data injection to sophisticated attack strategies:
Machine Learning-Based Detection: The survey by [3] highlights that ML techniques achieve 97–99% accuracy using the VeReMi dataset, with MA-CIDS(RF) achieving 97% accuracy and CIDS(SVM) achieving 99% F-measure. Haydari et al. [30] propose novel ML techniques for RSU-based online
intrusion detection, achieving 78% improvement over state-of-the-art solutions for false data injection and stealthy DDoS attacks when validated with real traffic datasets.
Deep Learning Approaches: Zhang et al. [4] employ deep learning for vehicle trust management in the AIT system, demonstrating superior accuracy and efficiency compared to existing approaches. Zheng et al. [2] integrate reward-shaping double duelling deep Q network (D3QN) learning in their
2PT-CIDS framework for privacy-aware anomaly detection and notification enhancement.
Federated Learning for Privacy-Preserving Detection: The blockchain-based federated learning scheme [28] enables collaborative training across distributed edge devices while ensuring data security and privacy. Differential privacy with the Gaussian mechanism provides strict privacy
protection for models on the blockchain, achieving satisfied accuracy and efficiency for common data falsification attacks. This approach addresses the critical challenge of centralized data collection and training in traditional misbehavior detection systems.
Hybrid ML Approaches: Tariq et al. [1] integrate ElasticNet and Gradient Boosting in the D-CASBR framework for real-time anomaly detection, achieving 95% accuracy with minimal false positives. The hybrid approach combines the strengths of different ML algorithms for robust detection.
Anomaly Detection Techniques: Liu et al. [16] propose time-aware (local reachability density, outlier factor) and space-aware (curl, divergence) anomaly detection in the DITVEN framework. Yang et al. [23] use affinity propagation for clustering-based anomaly detection, achieving a detection

Threshold-Based Detection: Many systems employ threshold-based classification after trust computation. Abhishek et al. [7], [25] use aggregated trust values compared against thresholds to classify RSUs as benign or malicious. Junejo et al. [6] implement a Decision Model that processes or
discards messages based on a trust threshold.
Game-Theoretic Detection: Zheng et al. [2] employ game-theoretic information incentive mechanisms (Stackelberg and noncooperative games) to prevent privacy leakage while enhancing anomaly detection, demonstrating the effectiveness of game theory for balancing security and privacy.
Collaborative Intrusion Detection Systems (CIDS): The survey [3] classifies CIDS approaches, noting that collaborative systems can achieve high accuracy but face challenges with communication overhead and real-time implementation. Zheng et al. [2] address these challenges with their two-
layer privacy-aware trust evaluation CIDS framework.
Key Findings: ML-based detection methods achieve high accuracy (95–99%) but most lack real-time implementation and real-world validation. Federated learning [28] and privacy-preserving approaches [2] address privacy concerns but introduce computational overhead. Lightweight, edge-
deployable ML models remain a critical gap. Most detection systems focus on vehicle misbehavior, with limited attention to RSU misbehavior detection [7], [10], [25].
Edge computing and RSU-based architectures enable low-latency, scalable misbehavior detection and trust management:
Fog-Enabled Architectures: Tariq et al. [1] propose a fog-enabled reputation system in the D-CASBR framework, eliminating single points of failure and addressing latency issues. Zhang et al. [11] use fog computing to analyze and store data for evaluating dynamic trust weights based on vehicle
attributes, achieving 12–40% higher successful service delivery and 10–20% higher effective network utility.
RSU-Based Detection: Multiple works leverage RSUs for centralized or semi-centralized detection. Haydari et al. [30] implement RSU-based online intrusion detection and mitigation, achieving superior detection performance with real dataset validation. Roy et al. [15] propose an edge cloud-based
privacy-preserving decision-making model using RSUs for traffic monitoring, validated with VENTOS, SUMO, and OMNeT++.
RSU Misbehavior Detection: A critical but underexplored area is detecting compromised RSUs. Abhishek et al. [7] propose a trust-based detection system where vehicles provide feedback to a central trusted server about RSU behavior. Siddiqua et al. [10], [13] use stable and frequent RSU-to-RSU
(R2R) interaction to analyze RSU behavior and detect alteration of sensor-detected data, achieving 92% recall and 86% accuracy with SUMO, NetEdit, and NS-3 simulations.
RSU-Managed Clusters: Alkhalidy et al. [22] organize vehicles into RSU-managed clusters, where RSUs assess node credibility before granting network access and evict malicious nodes based on trust values. This hierarchical approach reduces communication overhead and enables scalable trust
management.
Digital Twin Vehicular Edge Networks: Liu et al. [16] propose DITVEN to address time-space limitations in edge computing, establishing distributed trust evaluation based on trust chain transitivity and aggregation. The digital twin approach enables comprehensive data utilization beyond the
constraints of physical edge nodes.
Edge-Cloud Hybrid: Roy et al. [15] combine edge computing with cloud resources for privacy-preserving secured decision-making, demonstrating the benefits of hybrid architectures that balance low-latency edge processing with cloud-scale analytics.
RSU Gateway Architecture: Abhishek et al. [25] propose an architecture where vehicles report uplink and downlink trust values to an RSU gateway, which aggregates these values for threshold-based RSU classification. This centralized aggregation point enables coordinated detection across
multiple RSUs.
Key Findings: Edge/RSU architectures enable low-latency detection and scalable trust management. Fog-enabled systems [1], [11] achieve higher service delivery rates and network utility. However, RSU misbehavior detection remains underexplored, with most works assuming trusted RSUs. The
challenge of balancing centralized coordination (for global view) with distributed processing (for scalability and resilience) persists. Digital twin approaches [16] offer promising directions for overcoming edge computing limitations.
Software-Defined Networking (SDN) enables flexible, programmable control planes for VANETs, but the literature reveals limited integration of SDN with trust management and misbehavior detection:
Limited SDN Integration: Among the top 30 papers, explicit SDN integration is notably absent. Most works focus on distributed or semi-centralized architectures without leveraging SDN's programmability for adaptive security policies. This represents a significant gap, as SDN could enable
dynamic reconfiguration of trust thresholds, routing policies, and resource allocation in response to detected attacks.
Potential SDN Applications: While not explicitly implemented in the reviewed papers, several proposed mechanisms could benefit from SDN integration:
Dynamic Trust Threshold Adjustment: The HDRS system's [8] dynamic update intervals could be implemented as SDN flow rules, enabling centralized policy management with distributed enforcement.
Adaptive Routing: Zhang et al.'s [11] trust-aware heterogeneous traffic allocation could leverage SDN for dynamic path selection based on real-time trust assessments.
Isolation of Malicious Nodes: RSU misbehavior detection systems [7], [10], [25] could use SDN to immediately isolate detected malicious RSUs from the network control plane.
Control-Plane Resilience: The literature emphasizes distributed trust evaluation and decentralized architectures to avoid single points of failure [1], [16], but does not explicitly address SDN controller resilience. tolerance are critical considerations not addressed in the current literature.

Based on the comprehensive literature review, the following gaps and open problems are identified and prioritized:
1. Real-World Validation: Most approaches rely on simulation with synthetic data. Only Haydari et al. [30] validate with real traffic datasets. Real-world deployment and validation with actual vehicular networks are critically needed to assess practical performance, scalability, and robustness.
2. Lightweight Edge-Deployable ML Models: While ML-based detection achieves high accuracy (95–99%), most approaches do not address computational constraints of edge devices. Lightweight models optimized for RSU deployment with limited CPU, memory, and energy resources are
needed.
3. Integrated Trust Management and Secure Event Dissemination: Current works focus either on trust management or misbehavior detection, but lack integrated frameworks that combine trust-based validation with secure, reliable event dissemination mechanisms. How to ensure that only
trusted events are disseminated efficiently remains an open problem.
4. RSU Misbehavior Detection: While vehicle misbehavior detection is extensively studied, RSU misbehavior detection is underexplored [7], [10], [13], [25]. Compromised RSUs pose severe threats as they are trusted infrastructure nodes. Robust, scalable RSU misbehavior detection mechanisms
are critically needed.
5. Adaptive Trust Mechanisms for Dynamic Attack Scenarios: Most trust models use static thresholds or fixed update intervals. Dynamic, adaptive mechanisms that adjust trust parameters based on detected attack patterns and network conditions are needed. HDRS [8] and CARES [20] make
progress but require further development.
6. SDN Integration for Adaptive Security: The literature lacks integration of SDN with trust management and misbehavior detection. SDN's programmability could enable dynamic security policy adaptation, but controller resilience, placement, and Byzantine fault tolerance require investigation.
7. Privacy-Preserving Collaborative Detection at Scale: Federated learning [28] and privacy-aware CIDS [2] address privacy concerns, but scalability to thousands of vehicles and convergence time in highly dynamic networks remain challenges.
8. Sensor-Based Hazard Detection and Validation: Sensor-based pothole detection using accelerometer/IMU data is significantly underrepresented. Specific challenges include sensor noise, calibration differences, and distinguishing genuine hazards from other irregularities.
9. Multi-Attack Scenario Evaluation: Most works evaluate against single attack types. Comprehensive evaluation against multiple simultaneous attacks (e.g., false data injection + Sybil + collusion) is needed to assess robustness.
10. Blockchain Scalability and Latency: Blockchain-based approaches [4], [5], [28] provide strong security guarantees but face scalability and latency challenges. Lightweight blockchain protocols or hybrid approaches are needed for real-time VANET applications.
11. Cross-Layer Security Integration: Most approaches focus on application-layer trust and detection. Integration with lower-layer security mechanisms (MAC-layer authentication, physical-layer security) could provide defense-in-depth.
12. Standardization and Interoperability: Lack of standardized trust metrics, APIs, and protocols hinders interoperability between different trust management systems and VANET deployments.
13. Long-Term Trust Evolution: Most works focus on short-term trust assessment. Long-term trust evolution, reputation recovery mechanisms, and handling of rehabilitated nodes require further study.
14. Energy Efficiency: While some works mention lightweight designs [6], comprehensive energy efficiency analysis for battery-powered vehicles and solar-powered RSUs is limited.
15. Incentive Mechanisms: Beyond Shahariar et al.'s [14] fuzzy reward/punishment scheme, comprehensive incentive mechanisms to encourage honest reporting and participation in collaborative detection are underexplored.
